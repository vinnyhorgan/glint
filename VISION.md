# glint

## Philosophy

glint is a small fantasy machine for making 3D games with the feel of the late 90s: tactile, constrained, immediate, and a little dangerous in the best way.

It is not trying to be a general-purpose platform. It is trying to feel like a thing with a personality. Something closer to a beloved piece of hardware, a demo disc, or a shareware engine than a modern software stack.

The core feeling is **clarity**. You should be able to understand what the machine is doing, trust its boundaries, and get something visible on screen quickly. The project values:

- smallness over breadth
- mood over abstraction
- constraint over sprawl
- playfulness over polish-for-its-own-sake
- ownership over dependency on a giant engine

glint is meant to evoke the era of CRT glow, low-poly silhouettes, shimmering surfaces, noisy effects, and the kind of confidence that came from simple tools and hard limits.

It should feel like a place where one person can still make something real.

## Vibe

Think:

- demo-scene energy
- Quake-era grit
- 3dfx/Voodoo confidence
- shareware weirdness
- hardware that feels named, not branded

The name itself should carry a little shine, a little flicker, and a little nostalgia.

## What It Should Never Feel Like

- a bloated engine
- a corporate platform
- a modern productivity suite
- a taxonomy of features
- a blank framework waiting to be overdesigned

## In One Sentence

glint is a tiny retro-future fantasy PC for making evocative 3D worlds with the fewest moving parts possible.
